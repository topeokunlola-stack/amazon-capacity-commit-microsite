Who it is for

Built for

Frontier AI labs and large enterprises planning compute years ahead and seeking capacity, price, and delivery dates they can plan around.

Not built for

Demand that requires capacity to be built speculatively without a commitment. Burst remains best-effort at list price.

The offer

Three capacity tiers connect demand to delivery

The proposed tiers distinguish long-term committed demand from shorter-term flexibility and uncommitted use. All tiers use the same dashboard measures for utilisation, dates, and cost per unit.

Committed

3-year term, minimum use. Reserved capacity, locked price, delivery dates, and help moving qualifying workloads to custom chips.

Flexible

1-year term, lower minimum. Priority over Burst capacity, with an option to step up to Committed.

Burst

No commitment. Use capacity when available, at list price. Availability is best-effort.

How it works

1. Commit

Share a compute plan and sign a proposed term.

2. Plan

Receive a delivery schedule and migration plan for workloads that qualify.

3. Deliver

Track planned and delivered capacity in the dashboard.

4. Review

Review utilisation, cost per unit, and step-up options quarterly.

Capital-efficient capacity strategy

The core decision is to scale AWS and AI capacity only when contracted demand, silicon fit, financing visibility, and asset yield support the next tranche. Retail remains part of the existing flywheel; retail capital is released against payback evidence.

Goals and tradeoffs

Three goals

Base Case free cash flow positive by FY2028E; all-in capacity build at or under 100% of operating cash flow by FY2028E; AWS operating margin at least 33% while targeting growth near 33% a year.

Give up

Capacity built ahead of contracts and some uncommitted availability.

Keep

The retail flywheel, AWS growth, and the custom-silicon roadmap. New-market build-out and third-party Trainium sales are deferred in the proposal.

Four proposed capital tests

1. Contract cover

Backlog at least 3.0× trailing AWS revenue. The deck reports 3.3× in Q2 2026.

2. Silicon first

Use Trainium or Graviton where a workload qualifies; benchmark performance and cost before release.

3. Funding

All-in capacity build / operating cash flow no more than 120% in FY2027E and 100% in FY2028E. Count partner-funded capacity.

4. Yield and margin

Sales per dollar of property and equipment at least 0.50×; AWS operating margin at least 33%.

The gate levels are author-set starting points for calibration, not Amazon policy. A capacity tranche is released only when all four tests pass.

Operating model

The proposal uses a Capital Gate Committee with finance, AWS, infrastructure, and procurement representation, supported by six specialist functions: capacity planning, power and energy, silicon and supply chain, structured finance, gate analytics, and robotics reliability. The model assumes 160–275 new specialist roles across FY2027E–FY2028E; these staffing levels and costs are author estimates.

Risks and open checks

Backlog concentration

Backlog is company-reported and concentrated in a few AI labs that Amazon also invests in; its quality is not independently tested here.

Funding visibility

Partner structures can obscure capital costs. The proposal counts partner-funded capacity in the all-in build test.

Research limits

Desk research only; no customer interviews were conducted. Strategic investments and existing lease commitments are not modelled as cash flows.

What sets the proposed price

Commitment length

Longer terms support a deeper discount in the proposal.

Chip choice

Qualifying Trainium or Graviton workloads may reduce cost per unit; the performance claims need workload-level validation.

Minimum use

A higher minimum-use commitment supports a lower illustrative price.

Model limits

  • Tier prices, tier mix, account counts, staffing, AI tool costs, and gate thresholds are author assumptions.
  • The Base Case is a scenario, not a forecast; the Downside Case remains free-cash-flow negative through FY2028E.
